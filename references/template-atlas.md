# Template atlas

Use one template as the structural base. Combine at most two when their roles are complementary, such as a map structure plus a photographic window. Replace all sample copy with subject-specific wording.

## Contents

- Interwar geometry: T01–T06
- Mid-century wit: T07–T12
- Map and system: T13–T18
- Contemporary campaigns: T19–T24
- Multi-panel and special formats: T25–T30
- Digital and immersive systems: T31–T33

## Interwar geometry

### T01 — Extruded word junction

- Best for: energetic commands, roads, buses, launches, movement.
- Layout: oversized 3D headline lies across an angled platform or vehicle interior; letters pass behind poles, become trapped by closing doors, bridge a platform edge, or float just above a patterned floor.
- Type: cream or white faces, red or dark side extrusion, hard-edged perspective, compact auxiliary word.
- Palette: ochre or signal yellow, midnight navy, red shadow, turquoise accent, speckled grey.
- Finish: shallow isometric depth, screenprint grain, crisp hard-edged shadows.
- Avoid: photoreal CGI, many objects, or fully visible room architecture.

### T02 — Radial speed burst

- Best for: rapid service, commuting, sports, openings, express routes.
- Layout: one vehicle or abstract arrow shoots from a low vanishing point through 6–10 broad rays; headline anchors the calm top edge.
- Type: condensed capitals with generous tracking.
- Palette: black, warm cream, vermilion, cobalt, one pale blue.
- Finish: lithographic misregistration and slight ink bleed.
- Avoid: thin rays, lens flare, comic-book action lines.

### T03 — Clock and off-peak orbit

- Best for: time, schedules, night travel, event hours, countdowns.
- Layout: concentric clock rings or orbital discs dominate; two wedges mark the meaningful interval; copy follows an arc or sits in two quiet bands.
- Type: geometric sans integrated into the dial.
- Palette: dusty pink, powder blue, black, parchment, one vivid red.
- Finish: flat colour and paper tooth.
- Avoid: a literal detailed clock face unless time accuracy is essential.

### T04 — Monumental flat silhouette

- Best for: destinations, exhibitions, parks, architecture, seasonal outings.
- Layout: one cropped figure, building, flower, or object fills 70% of the sheet as two to five interlocking colour masses; destination line sits below.
- Type: small but firm title block, widely spaced.
- Palette: four opaque inks plus paper colour.
- Finish: poster-paint flats with no outlines and almost no shading.
- Avoid: miniature skylines and descriptive detail.

### T05 — Cubist power grid

- Best for: technology, electricity, network capacity, innovation.
- Layout: cables, rails, pistons, or station parts become a Cubist lattice around a bright central node; headline is a stable counterweight.
- Type: small modernist block, not integrated into the lattice.
- Palette: charcoal, rust, teal, cream, electric yellow.
- Finish: angular gouache, dry-brush edges, compressed depth.
- Avoid: cyberpunk glow or circuit-board literalism.

### T06 — Art Deco night aperture

- Best for: theatre, nightlife, winter evenings, cultural destinations.
- Layout: a dark architectural arch frames one luminous destination; stepped rays or window bands pull inward.
- Type: elegant high-waist geometric capitals.
- Palette: navy-black, plum, warm gold, cream, optional coral.
- Finish: velvety ink, restrained metallic suggestion, no actual foil unless requested.
- Avoid: ornate Gatsby decoration and excessive symmetry.

## Mid-century wit

### T07 — Single visual pun emblem

- Best for: service benefits, polite behaviour, reliability, value.
- Layout: one familiar transit object transforms into an unexpected symbol; large empty field; two-line explanation below.
- Type: friendly humanist sans, sentence case.
- Palette: cream field, black, red, service blue, one comic accent.
- Finish: clean screenprint with one rough edge.
- Avoid: explaining the pun twice or adding secondary icons.

### T08 — Landmark totem

- Best for: sightseeing, neighbourhood identity, festivals.
- Layout: three to five landmark fragments stack into one vertical totem that also resembles a vehicle, ticket, or letterform.
- Type: compact destination at top, route instruction at foot.
- Palette: brick red, fog grey, leaf green, mustard, ink blue.
- Finish: cut-paper blocks and subtle halftone.
- Avoid: souvenir-shop icon arrays.

### T09 — Cut-paper excursion window

- Best for: parks, river trips, family days, weekend exploration.
- Layout: a geometric carriage window reveals a simplified outdoor scene; the window frame doubles as the poster border.
- Type: headline on the interior wall, destination line below the window.
- Palette: soft green, sky blue, cream, black, one warm red.
- Finish: layered paper fibres and shallow shadows.
- Avoid: realistic landscape painting.

### T10 — Mosaic destination field

- Best for: museums, music, multicultural events, placemaking.
- Layout: tessellated pieces form a recognisable subject only at distance; one route-colour path crosses the field.
- Type: quiet label in a solid corner tile.
- Palette: six muted ceramic colours with one bright route hue.
- Finish: printed mosaic or linocut, not glossy ceramic render.
- Avoid: tiny equal-sized tiles and decorative noise.

### T11 — Botanical screenprint invitation

- Best for: gardens, spring, clean air, walking, outdoor events.
- Layout: one plant form grows around a platform edge, handrail, or route line; headline occupies open sky.
- Type: sturdy mixed case with generous leading.
- Palette: leaf green, citron, coral, deep blue, uncoated white.
- Finish: overlapping transparent inks and visible screen texture.
- Avoid: botanical-science detail and floral borders.

### T12 — Negative-space object

- Best for: safety, quietness, accessibility, civic messages.
- Layout: a large flat object contains a second transit image in its negative space; footer is narrow and factual.
- Type: bold short headline, small support line.
- Palette: two strong colours, black, and paper.
- Finish: razor-sharp silhouette with mild ink wear.
- Avoid: gradients and ambiguous figure-ground relationships.

## Map and system

### T13 — Source-object route map

- Best for: art, food, music, creativity, themed journeys.
- Layout: routes visibly emerge from a source object such as a paint tube, instrument, book, cup, or cable reel; nodes become subject-specific details.
- Type: destination title in map-label style but not a copy of Tube typography.
- Palette: five route colours on warm white, with black labels.
- Finish: crisp vector lines over a lightly printed object.
- Avoid: copying the real Tube map or station names wholesale.

### T14 — Route ribbon landscape

- Best for: cross-city journeys, green routes, river services, walking.
- Layout: one broad coloured ribbon bends through a stripped landscape and changes role as road, river, rail, and horizon.
- Type: headline follows one straight segment; details in a bottom band.
- Palette: one dominant service hue, two landscape neutrals, one contrast.
- Finish: flat gouache and long clean curves.
- Avoid: thin spaghetti lines or perspective inconsistency.

### T15 — Node constellation

- Best for: community, connection, nighttime service, technology.
- Layout: 8–14 nodes form both a network and a hidden subject silhouette; three larger interchange nodes establish hierarchy.
- Type: minimal labels, generous open field.
- Palette: near-black field, ivory, cyan, orange, magenta accent.
- Finish: screenprinted dots and short line segments, no glow bloom.
- Avoid: data-visualisation complexity.

### T16 — Compass quarters

- Best for: citywide campaigns, four destinations, seasonal choices.
- Layout: sheet divides into four unequal directional fields around a central interchange; each contains one simplified motif.
- Type: NORTH / SOUTH / EAST / WEST or user-specific alternatives.
- Palette: four coordinated inks plus dark text.
- Finish: flat print with subtle overprint where fields meet.
- Avoid: equal corporate quadrants; keep one direction dominant.

### T17 — Ticket geometry

- Best for: fares, admission, events, nostalgia, limited editions.
- Layout: enlarged ticket fragments, perforations, dates, and route codes become abstract architecture; one literal detail confirms the idea.
- Type: monospaced numerals plus humanist headline.
- Palette: faded buff, orange, navy, green, black.
- Finish: letterpress dents, worn edges, registration marks.
- Avoid: fake financial claims or scannable real barcodes.

### T18 — Moquette rhythm

- Best for: comfort, belonging, heritage, music, fashion.
- Layout: a transit-seat pattern expands into city movement; one break in the repeat reveals the focal message.
- Type: simple solid panel or letters knocked out of the pattern.
- Palette: dark blue base with two bright woven accents and beige.
- Finish: woven texture translated into bold printed loops.
- Avoid: reproducing a protected real moquette pattern exactly.

## Contemporary campaigns

### T19 — Civic portrait mosaic

- Best for: inclusion, staff stories, community, anniversaries.
- Layout: one to three truthful portraits anchor the lower half; abstract route shapes and short quotations weave around them.
- Type: warm sentence-case headline, restrained biography line.
- Palette: natural skin tones plus red, blue, pink, and cream accents.
- Finish: editorial photography or clean illustration with a unifying print grain.
- Avoid: tokenistic crowds and unreadable testimonial text.

### T20 — Friendly service characters

- Best for: etiquette, family travel, accessibility, app features.
- Layout: two or three simplified characters interact with one oversized transport object; a speech-like headline occupies the negative space.
- Type: rounded humanist sans.
- Palette: bright but controlled red, blue, yellow, mint, cream.
- Finish: flat vector shapes, slight paper grain, expressive gestures.
- Avoid: childish mascot overload and literal speech bubbles everywhere.

### T21 — Typographic command in motion

- Best for: safety, behaviour, walking, cycling, launches.
- Layout: a 2–5 word command becomes the motion itself: letters bend as track, queue, barrier, steps, road, doorway, or handrail; preserve the reading order through every distortion.
- Type: oversized custom block lettering with flat faces and selective extrusion, outline, overlap, or colour blocking; one small explanatory line.
- Palette: one high-chroma field, white or cream letters, dark shadow, one signal colour.
- Finish: clean contemporary print with one tactile layer.
- Avoid: generic kinetic-type distortion that destroys reading.

### T22 — Isometric vehicle micro-world

- Best for: mode launches, new interiors, station improvements, product stories.
- Layout: a cropped bus, train, platform, escalator, or street is shown from a first-person or slightly elevated oblique viewpoint; seats, poles, windows, doors, or headline objects reveal scale.
- Type: integrated into floor, roof, platform, glazing, or vehicle geometry.
- Palette: ochre, deep blue, turquoise, red, concrete grey.
- Finish: shallow isometric illustration, grainy screenprint, simplified materials.
- Avoid: architectural completeness and photoreal product rendering.

### T23 — Photographic window plus colour field

- Best for: real destinations, neighbourhood campaigns, documentary stories.
- Layout: one honest rectangular or torn-edge photograph occupies 35–55%; a large colour field carries the headline and one source-derived abstract motif.
- Type: quiet editorial hierarchy.
- Palette: sample two colours from the photograph, add dark ink and warm paper.
- Finish: real photo remains unfiltered; surrounding field uses paper and ink texture.
- Avoid: fake film filters and many photo tiles.

### T24 — One-hue civic illustration

- Best for: environmental messages, public realm, seasonal campaigns.
- Layout: a spacious city scene is compressed into three large forms; one saturated hue acts as route, shadow, and graphic structure.
- Type: small top headline and factual footline.
- Palette: one vivid hue, two neutrals, black, paper.
- Finish: hand-cut or brush-built shapes with active negative space.
- Avoid: dense foliage, tiny pedestrians, and tourism-detail clutter.

## Multi-panel and special formats

### T25 — Sequential triptych

- Best for: three steps, before/during/after, three modes.
- Layout: three portrait posters each show one stage; a single route line continues across all panels.
- Type: same position and size, changing only the verb.
- Palette: constant neutrals with one changing accent per panel.
- Finish: one shared print method.
- Avoid: three unrelated posters.

### T26 — Day and night pair

- Best for: all-day service, culture, hospitality, seasonal hours.
- Layout: same composition twice; light, activity, and secondary copy change while geometry remains locked.
- Type: identical headline grid.
- Palette: warm daylight pair versus indigo and luminous cream.
- Finish: paired lithographs with matching grain.
- Avoid: merely applying a dark filter.

### T27 — Destination series system

- Best for: boroughs, museums, parks, stations, route stops.
- Layout: fixed top title and footer; each poster substitutes one monumental subject cropped at the same scale.
- Type: consistent family; destination name is the variable hero.
- Palette: shared dark ink and paper, rotating two-colour pair.
- Finish: consistent screenprint or cut-paper treatment.
- Avoid: changing template family between destinations.

### T28 — Accessible journey diagram

- Best for: inclusive service concepts, step-free stories, wayfinding education.
- Layout: one simple human journey across three generous zones, with barriers visually removed or bridged.
- Type: plain-language headline and three short labels.
- Palette: high contrast; sparing colour; never rely on colour alone.
- Finish: clear flat illustration with large symbols.
- Avoid: pretending to provide current official accessibility information.

### T29 — Digital motion keyframe

- Best for: digital escalator panels, launch loops, social motion concepts.
- Layout: 16:9 or tall digital frame with one element captured mid-transition; keep a clean first and final reading state in the same composition.
- Type: maximum five words, readable within two seconds.
- Palette: dark field and two luminous flat colours.
- Finish: motion implied by repeated printed shapes, not blur.
- Avoid: tiny footers and rapid strobing stripe patterns.

### T30 — Minimal service notice

- Best for: conceptual alerts, concise facts, dates, calls to action.
- Layout: 65% blank field, one symbolic object, one headline, one clearly separated detail block.
- Type: mixed case, strong spacing, no more than three sizes.
- Palette: cream, dark navy, one signal colour.
- Finish: exceptionally clean with light paper tooth.
- Avoid: resemblance to a live operational alert; label fictional concepts clearly.

## Digital and immersive systems

### T31 — LED ribbon gateway

- Best for: launches, digital station gateways, night service, future mobility.
- Layout: two or three broad luminous ribbons sweep through a dark architectural aperture and form one stable central reading zone.
- Type: short flat headline held in true-black negative space; never set small copy into the light stream.
- Palette: electric blue, magenta, neon coral, ultraviolet, indigo-black.
- Finish: LED-native luminosity, restrained pixel mesh, controlled bloom, crisp still-frame hierarchy.
- Avoid: official roundel construction, rainbow overload, and generic screensaver waves.

### T32 — Anamorphic tunnel word

- Best for: digital out-of-home spectacle, mode launches, short behavioural commands.
- Layout: a 2–4 word headline appears to project from or recede into a tunnel, platform edge, or screen corner; the illusion must also read as a flat keyframe.
- Type: one monumental word-object with simple faces and high-contrast side planes.
- Palette: one dark field, one luminous face colour, one warm accent, white only if needed.
- Finish: precise perspective, shallow pixel texture, no photoreal chrome.
- Avoid: unreadable distortion, multiple vanishing points, and copying a live campaign.

### T33 — Data-reactive ambient network

- Best for: fictional weather, crowd, energy, or cultural-data concepts.
- Layout: 8–15 nodes or pulses alter the density, colour, or rhythm of one clear network motif; reserve a calm message zone.
- Type: one short phrase and at most one fictional status label.
- Palette: deep indigo, cyan, coral, cream, optional lime signal.
- Finish: generative logic expressed as broad shapes rather than dashboard detail.
- Avoid: presenting real-time claims, fake accessibility information, and data-viz clutter.
