# Campaign recipes

Use these recipes to move quickly from a loose request to a generation-ready brief. Each recipe points back to the template atlas; replace bracketed variables and rewrite generic language into concrete visual instructions. Do not put artist or agency names into the image-generation prompt. Translate references into observable traits.

## R1 — Dimensional-type safety and behaviour

Best templates: T01 + T21, optionally T22 for the transport interior.

Use for short safety, etiquette, walking, cycling, or service-awareness messages. Make the letters the event rather than placing type over an illustration.

```text
Create one high-impact 2:3 London public-transport-inspired poster for [MESSAGE PURPOSE].
Show a cropped [BUS / TRAIN / PLATFORM / ESCALATOR] from a [FIRST-PERSON / SLIGHTLY ELEVATED] perspective.
Render "[2–5 WORD PHRASE]" exactly once as massive geometric letters with white or cream faces and [RED / NAVY] extruded sides. Integrate the letters physically into the scene: [TRAPPED BY DOORS / PIERCED BY A POLE / BRIDGING THE GAP / FOLLOWING THE ESCALATOR / RESTING ON THE FLOOR].
Use saturated ochre or yellow, deep blue, red, cream, and one cyan accent. Add restrained stipple or screenprint grain and hard-edged shallow shadows.
Keep one dominant idea, 10% safe margin, strong thumbnail legibility, and no more than one support line.
Exclude official TfL marks, exact wayfinding, real service claims, glossy CGI, extra text, and copied campaign composition.
```

Scene starters: closing carriage doors, bus seat and vertical pole, platform edge, escalator vanishing point, vehicle window band, yellow exterior panel.

## R2 — Classic modernist destination

Best templates: T02, T04, T06, T08, T09, or T11.

Use for landmarks, gardens, theatres, museums, riverside, countryside, or a neighbourhood. Choose one monumental subject, not a skyline collage.

```text
Create one 2:3 destination poster inspired by historic London public-transport modernism, recomposed as an original work.
Turn [DESTINATION] into one monumental graphic subject using [ART DECO RAYS / FLAT COLOUR MASSES / CUT PAPER / GEOMETRIC SILHOUETTE].
Render "[DESTINATION OR SLOGAN]" exactly once in bold geometric or humanist sans lettering, with one small fictional travel line.
Use [SEASON / TIME OF DAY] light, four opaque inks, warm uncoated paper, and a clear invitation to travel.
Exclude literal postcard collage, exact vintage composition, official logos, Tube map, and generic sepia distress.
```

## R3 — Cultural venue invitation

Best templates: T04, T10, T13, T19, or T23.

Use for galleries, exhibitions, music, theatres, festivals, or architecture. Let the venue's content generate the transport metaphor.

```text
Create one contemporary 2:3 London transport-inspired cultural poster for [VENUE / EVENT].
Show [ONE RECOGNISABLE VENUE FEATURE OR ART FORM] as bold simplified forms; transform [PAINT / MUSIC / TEXTILE / STAGE LIGHT / BOOK] into a route, interchange, window, or vehicle structure.
Render "[VENUE NAME]" exactly once as the headline and "[SHORT FICTIONAL TRAVEL CUE]" once as support.
Use rich colour blocks, clean modern illustration, classic poster hierarchy, subtle print grain, and generous negative space.
Exclude artist-style imitation, logo copying, literal event-photo montage, and extra text.
```

## R4 — Seasonal atmosphere

Best templates: T06, T09, T11, T14, T24, or T26.

Use for spring, summer, autumn, winter, weather, night travel, and holiday-adjacent campaigns. Make the season alter the transit structure rather than decorate it.

```text
Create one 2:3 seasonal London public-transport-inspired poster for [SEASON / MOMENT].
Use [BLOSSOM / FALLING LEAVES / SNOW / SUMMER RIVER LIGHT / LONG EVENING SHADOW] to become [ROUTE / WINDOW / PLATFORM EDGE / ESCALATOR RHYTHM].
Render "[2–6 WORD SEASONAL PHRASE]" exactly once with elegant, highly legible display type and one short support line.
Use a soft but saturated limited palette, broad quiet forms, tactile paper, and a strong sense of journey and place.
Exclude decorative borders, many landmarks, low contrast, and generic greeting-card imagery.
```

## R5 — Abstract power and modernity

Best templates: T02, T03, T05, T14, or T15.

Use for speed, electricity, network capacity, technology, construction, and future mobility.

```text
Create one bold 2:3 abstract London transport-inspired poster about [POWER / SPEED / NETWORK / TECHNOLOGY].
Build a dynamic composition from [RAYS / CUBIST CABLES / NODES / BROAD ROUTE RIBBONS / ORBITS] around one bright central action.
Render "[2–5 WORD POWER SLOGAN]" exactly once as large stable geometric type that counterbalances the motion.
Use high-contrast red, blue, yellow, charcoal, and warm paper with opaque lithographic inks and slight misregistration.
Exclude cyberpunk glow, circuit-board literalism, thin data lines, official maps, and visual clutter.
```

## R6 — Moquette and pattern-led message

Best templates: T18, optionally combined with T21.

Use for heritage, comfort, rhythm, music, fashion, or belonging. Invent a new transit-seat rhythm rather than reproducing a real protected pattern.

```text
Create one 2:3 pattern-led London transport-inspired poster.
Invent a bold woven rhythm from [LOOPS / ZIGZAGS / ROUTE CURVES / INTERLOCKING NODES] in deep blue, red, green, and cream. Let one deliberate break in the repeat reveal "[MESSAGE]" exactly once as [FLAT KNOCKOUT / SHALLOW 3D TYPE / SOLID PANEL].
Keep the repeat large, graphic, rhythmic, and readable from a distance; show subtle textile-to-screenprint texture.
Exclude copied moquette, tiny high-frequency stripes, official marks, and decorative pattern with no hierarchy.
```

## R7 — Promotional service message

Best templates: choose T07 or T12 for a witty benefit, T21/T22 for a punchy launch, T28 for an inclusive journey concept, or T30 for a minimal notice.

Use for fictional fares, night service, new modes, accessibility concepts, apps, cleaner travel, or line extensions. Do not invent apparently current operational facts.

```text
Create one 2:3 London public-transport-inspired promotional poster for [FICTIONAL OR CLEARLY CONCEPTUAL SERVICE].
Communicate one benefit through [VISUAL PUN / DIMENSIONAL HEADLINE / SIMPLE JOURNEY DIAGRAM / MINIMAL SYMBOL].
Render "[2–6 WORD MESSAGE]" exactly once and "[SHORT SUPPORT LINE]" exactly once.
Use high contrast, clean geometric illustration, one dominant transport scene or object, and a compact footer reading "UNOFFICIAL CONCEPT".
Exclude real fares, closures, schedules, accessibility status, copied branding, and dense body copy.
```

## R8 — Digital and immersive campaign

Best templates: T29, T31, T32, or T33.

Use for digital escalator panels, platform-edge screens, LED gateways, motion loops, fictional data-reactive art, and future mobility. Design the first frame to work as a still image before adding motion.

```text
Create one [9:16 / 16:9] London public-transport-inspired digital campaign keyframe for [THEME].
Build the composition from [LUMINOUS ROUTE RIBBONS / PIXEL-MESH PLANES / ANAMORPHIC TYPE / DATA-REACTIVE NODES] moving through a deep architectural field. Keep one stable two-second reading state and one clear focal transition.
Render "[2–5 WORD MESSAGE]" exactly once in minimal highly legible sans-serif type.
Use electric blue, magenta, ultraviolet, neon coral, and deep indigo-black with controlled LED luminosity and true black negative space.
Make the work feel immersive and spatial while retaining broad transport-poster geometry at thumbnail size.
Exclude an official roundel, live service data, strobing high-frequency stripes, uncontrolled glow bloom, cyberpunk clutter, tiny labels, and extra text.
```

## Variation menu

After a successful first poster, suggest at most three changes that produce a real compositional difference:

- Colour: switch the dominant field, not every colour.
- Viewpoint: first-person interior, elevated isometric, platform-level, or exterior crop.
- Texture: clean vector, light stipple, opaque lithograph, screenprint grain, or cut paper.
- Type: flat colour block, shallow red-sided extrusion, heavy architectural extrusion, outline plus fill, or letters transformed into route geometry.

For a 3–4 poster series, lock two of these axes and vary only the third plus the scene. Keep headline position, footer grid, palette logic, and text depth consistent.
